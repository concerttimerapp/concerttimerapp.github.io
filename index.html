What it does

Everything the evening asks of you, and nothing else.

Concert Timer was written backstage, for the person holding the stopwatch. It does the small number of things that job actually requires — and it does them in the dark, in a hurry, without asking you to think about the app.

  1. 01 Build a program in seconds Pieces, intermission, conductor and soloists. Drag to reorder; duplicate a program for the second night.
  2. 02 Time each piece live One large, legible clock. Turn the phone sideways and it fills the screen — readable from across the stage.
  3. 03 From your wrist Start, pause and stop on your Apple Watch while the iPhone stays in your pocket. Each press counts from the instant you make it, and the current piece is on your watch face.
  4. 04 Countdown intermission Adjustable length, a warning before it ends, and a tuning A at zero. Choose your pitch, from Baroque (392, 415 Hz) to modern (440–443 Hz).
  5. 05 Encores, on the fly Add a piece the moment you see the soloist sit back down. Name it once you recognise what they are playing.
  6. 06 A mistaken Stop, undone A break between movements can sound like an ending. The back arrow reopens the piece with every second since the Stop added back — even after the next piece has started.
  7. 07 A PDF, immediately The complete timing sheet — scheduled start, every duration, the total, and the time the concert actually ended.
  8. 08 Nothing leaves your phone No account, no sign-in, no analytics, no cloud. The concerts are yours and they stay on the device.

The timing sheet

Sent before the audience has left the hall.

The personnel manager needs the timings tonight; the archivist needs them tomorrow. When the concert ends the PDF is already written — dated, titled, and set in the same quiet typography as the app.

Share it by mail, message or AirDrop without leaving the screen.
